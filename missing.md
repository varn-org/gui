# Missing — what is reported, what is fixed, what is still open

Nothing leaves this file until it is built, held by a test, and written into `docs/review.md`.

This is the round of 2026-09-14, in two halves. The first is a validation of everything the three rounds before it built, which is sections AT, AU, AV and AW of `docs/review.md`. The second is five applications that read as the ones people actually have on their phones, built out of nothing but this library.

## Part one: what the validation found

- [x] **1. Every check the library keeps of itself runs clean.** The eleven promise checks — a node type no renderer builds, a colour nobody paints, a prop the engine never resolves, a setter nothing calls, a type no renderer can build, a promise nobody keeps, a name nothing reads, a method declared twice, a prop answered twice, an action nobody performs, a capability named two ways — all answer nothing. Every file named anywhere in the docs exists. No sentence in any document is split by a semicolon.

- [x] **2. A control the platform draws was being drawn by the engine anyway.** `Card` and `Tooltip` registered their kinds with an entry of their own, which is what makes `native` leave a kind alone — right for a control no system has, wrong for one every system has. Both were drawn by the engine under every control theme, so the platform's own card and tooltip were never reached at all. Each is a kind with no default now and the three drawn themes carry an entry for it. Held by a case that reads every control under `native` and under `material3` and fails on one drawn the same way by both, which is the shape of the defect rather than the instance of it.

- [x] **3. Every control draws under every control theme.** Sixteen controls by four themes, each built and laid out, none raising. Held by the same case.

## Part two: five applications

Each is a whole application rather than a screen: a reader opens it, moves through it and comes back. None carries a real name or a real mark, and each is built out of this library and nothing else — no photographs fetched from anywhere, no fonts beyond the one the framework ships, and nothing a renderer cannot draw.

What makes one of these read as the real thing is not the colour. It is the density, the proportions, the order things are in, and the particular shapes each of those products settled on. So each entry says what those are.

- [x] **4. A food delivery application.** `sample/apps/plate.lua`. Red as its one strong colour against white. The home screen is a header carrying the delivery address as a pressable row with a chevron, a search field, a scrolling row of round category marks with their names under them, a band of offers as wide cards with a discount flag on the corner, then the restaurant list — each row a square picture on the left, the name, the rating out of five as a number with a mark beside it, the kind of food, the distance, the delivery time as a range, and the delivery fee as either a price or the word for free in green. A restaurant screen: the cover, the name, the rating, the delivery line, a row of tabs for the sections of the menu, then the dishes as rows with a description over two lines and a price. A dish screen with a quantity stepper and an add button carrying the running total. A bag screen, and an address screen behind the header.

- [x] **5. A ride-hailing application.** `sample/apps/hail.lua`. Black and white, with the map as the whole screen. The home screen is the map with a card raised over the bottom of it: a field for where to, a row of recent places each with a mark and two lines, and a row of saved ones. Choosing one opens the ride screen: the map with a route on it and a panel listing the kinds of car — each a row with a drawing of the car, the name, how many it takes, when it arrives and the price, the chosen one outlined. Then the trip screen: the driver, the car, the plate, the rating and what a rider can do. The map is drawn rather than fetched, since a tile server is a dependency and a drawn map is a map.

- [x] **6. A marketplace application.** `sample/apps/bazaar.lua`. Yellow and blue, dense, the busiest of the five. The home screen: a search field across the top with a barcode mark, a row of small category circles, a carousel of offers, a band of the day's deals as a scrolling row of cards each with the picture, the discount as a percentage in green, the old price struck through and the new one, then a grid of recommendations two across. A product screen: the picture carousel with dots, the title in full, the price with the instalments under it, the free delivery line in green, the seller, the stock, and two buttons — buy now and add to the basket. A search results screen with a filter row. A basket.

- [x] **7. A messaging application.** `sample/apps/sprig.lua`. Green, and the one screen everybody knows: a list of conversations, each a round picture, the name, the last message on one line, the time on the right, and an unread count as a green pill. A conversation screen with the bubbles — the reader's own in a pale green against the right, the other side in white against the left, each with the time inside the bottom corner and the read marks on the reader's own — a wallpaper behind them, and a composer with a mark for attachments, the field, and a mark that turns from a microphone into a send arrow as soon as there is something to send. Tabs across the top for the conversations, the status and the calls.

- [x] **8. An audiobook application.** `sample/apps/lull.lua`. Dark, with the covers doing the work. A home screen of shelves: continue listening, what is new, and a row per category, each a horizontal scroll of covers with the title and the author under them. A book screen: the cover large, the title, the author, the narrator, how long it runs, a play button and the chapters as a list. A player screen: the cover filling the top, the title, the scrubber with the elapsed and remaining time, the transport row with back fifteen and on thirty, the speed and the sleep timer. A library and a search behind the two marks in the header.

## What every one of them owes

- [x] **9. Every screen is reachable and every screen comes back.** Each application is a `gui.NavigationStack`, so every screen a reader can open they can leave. Where an application's own band is the point — the red one, the green one, the yellow one — the screen says `hidesBar` for itself and draws it, which is what made `hidesBar` and `barStyle` per-screen rather than per-stack: nearly every application on a phone is a first screen carrying its own header and detail screens under the platform's bar, and a stack that could only be all one or all the other could not draw either of them.

- [x] **10. Nothing is fetched and nothing is a photograph.** Thirty-nine pictures — six dishes, six wide covers built from them, six shopfront marks, three cars, six goods, six book covers and six faces — are drawn by `tools/draw-scenes.py` from the shape of the thing each stands for, in the palette its own application uses. The map and the wallpaper are not files at all: each is a `gui.Canvas` worked out from the box it turned out to fill, so neither is a size to ship or a picture to scale.

- [x] **11. No real name, no real mark.** None of the five carries the name, the logo or the exact colour of the product it is built after. `gui/tests/sample_test.lua` holds every source file and every page in `docs/` against a list of thirty-two names, matched as whole words rather than as runs of letters, since several of them sit inside ordinary English — a paragraph about wrapping text carries one and is a paragraph about wrapping text.

- [x] **12. Every one of them is held by the suite.** `gui/tests/apps_test.lua` walks each of the five: it presses what a reader would press, reads what would then be on screen and what would no longer be, checks what can be reached by a reader who cannot see it, and then unwinds the whole stack and checks where it lands.

- [x] **13. Every one of them keeps the guidelines.** `gui/tests/guidelines_test.lua` walks the whole gallery, so nothing smaller than a finger, nothing that can be pressed and says nothing to a reader who cannot see it, and no handler with an empty body. It caught the marketplace's own header twice, at forty points where a finger needs forty-four.

- [x] **14. The screenshots.** A demo may name what to press to reach the screens inside it and what to file each picture under, so the browser walks into each application and writes a picture of every screen rather than of the one it opens on. That is what found a restaurant cover that was its own logo stretched across a banner, two rows of carousel dots, a slider a third of the width it had, a paperclip that came out as an O, and a menu whose dishes ran together with no rule between them. `run.py shots --platform ios` takes the same sweep on the simulator and is unchanged by any of this — it was left running at the end of this round and had not got past building the host, which is the environment rather than the code.

- [x] **15. The docs.** `docs/sample.md` for what the sample carries and why, linked from the README, and `CLAUDE.md` for what building them established.

## What the five needed that the library did not have

Each of these is here because an application wanted it, rather than because it seemed like a good idea.

- [x] **16. A subtree in a look of its own.** `gui.Look` draws everything under it in another look and leaves the rest of the application in its own. Five applications in one gallery is five palettes in one tree, and the only way to change a look was to change the whole runtime's. Every colour, spacing step and corner is resolved on the way out of the runtime, so this is the runtime knowing which look governs which node: the index walk it already makes every commit stamps each node with the look in force, and what resolves a style reads that rather than the application's. A subtree told to wear another look is noticed where it happens and the tree is painted again, so a `Look` whose value changes reaches every node under it without any of them rendering. Held by `gui/tests/theme_test.lua`, which draws two boxes in one tree and checks they come out different colours, and then turns the device dark and checks both sides followed.

- [x] **17. A field carried a box of its own on two platforms out of three.** An application draws a search pill and puts an editable run inside it, and on the web and on Android the run arrived with the user agent's border, ground and padding, so the pill had a second box sitting in it. iOS had none, so the same screen was two different screens. What the platform owns is the caret, the selection and the keyboard, and the box around them belongs to whoever wrote the screen — which is what `TextInput`'s own default style has always said. The web renderer's comment already promised this and only did it for a button.

- [x] **18. Fourteen marks the set did not carry.** A bag, a cart, a barcode, a filter, a microphone, a paperclip, a phone, a video camera, a shield, a rewind, a forward, a list, a double check and a headset. A messenger whose composer has no microphone and a marketplace whose search has no barcode are not copies of anything. The gallery draws the whole set from `icons.names()`, so each appears there without being listed anywhere.

- [x] **19. A list of children spread anywhere but last.** `table.unpack` in the middle of a table constructor yields one value rather than all of them, so a row of five cars written before a button is one car and a button — no nil, no error, and nothing on screen wrong enough to notice. It has now cost a stepper its keys, a checkbox and a radio their focus rings, and a ride screen four of its five rows. `promises.unspread()` reads every Lua file under `gui` and `sample` and fails on a spread that is not the last thing written in its element, so it is a failing suite rather than a screen somebody eventually looks at.

- [x] **20. A page nothing linked to.** `docs/controls.md` was written, tested and reachable only by knowing its filename. The README index is what a reader arrives at, so `gui/tests/components_test.lua` fails on any page in `docs/` the README does not name.

- [x] **21. A slider was a third of the width it had and its thumb never left the left end.** Both found by looking at a screen, and neither is anything a tree-based case could see: the engine sent the right frame and the right value both times. A range input takes whole numbers until it is told otherwise, so a slider from nought to one could only ever be at either end. And a slider and a segmented control each answer a width of their own when a platform is asked how large it draws one, while both actually run the width of the row they sit in — so the engine believed one platform, the other two said `noIntrinsicMetric`, and the exception that hid it was written inside one renderer for one of the two controls. Which axes a platform decides is the declaration's to say now, and the stand-in that let the old case pass answers what a platform answers rather than what the exception produced.
